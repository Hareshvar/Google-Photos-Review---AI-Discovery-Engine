Among 9,546 user reports, 4,443 (46.54 %) involve **Document or Receipt**, 2,818 (29.52 %) are **Other photo type**, 1,451 (15.20 %) are **Person or pet photo**, 605 (6.34 %) are tied to **Exact Date & Time**, 124 (1.30 %) to **Event Context**, 103 (1.08 %) to **Remembered Location**, and only 2 (0.02 %) to **Specific object or item**. Q2: What information do people actually remember about a photo?
SUMMARY: The question measures the specific cues users rely on to retrieve a photo after a retrieval failure. Among 9,774 responses, 5,349 (54.73 %) recalled **Event Context**, 1,948 (19.93 %) recalled **Exact Date & Time**, 1,490 (15.24 %) recalled a **Person or Face**, 667 (6.82 %) recalled **Location**, 317 (3.24 %) recalled **Text Inside Photo (OCR)**, and only 1 (0.01 %) recalled **Exact Text Search**, **Quotation Marks**, or other specific cues. Verbatim quotes illustrate friction: users complain that face recognition merges unrelated images, that community photos do not display all faces, and that they cannot remember a cafe name to search, highlighting gaps in contextual and textual search functionality. These findings suggest prioritizing robust event tagging, accurate date handling, and improved face grouping to match the most frequently remembered cues.

Q8: Does the user struggle to refine an unsuccessful search?
SUMMARY: The question measures how frequently users, after an unsuccessful search, attempt to refine their query or explore alternative methods before abandoning the task. Among 9,774 feedback instances, 95.44 % (9,328) indicate users **Scrolled Main Timeline**, 3.94 % (385) show users **Switched to Competitor / Other Apps**, and 0.75 % (73) reflect users **Gave Up Search**. The dominance of scrolling suggests that users primarily rely on manual browsing rather than refining search terms, while the switch and give‑up rates reveal friction points where search refinement is insufficient. Verbatim evidence—such as users noting missing screenshots, wanting dedicated folders, and expressing frustration with Drive integration—highlights that the current refinement options do not meet expectations, leading to continued scrolling or abandonment.
